[#f5e342]Origin[-]

The griffin, with the body of a lion and the head and wings of an eagle, is one of the oldest monsters of all: it appears in Persian and Greek art long before any book. The Greek historian Herodotus passed on the story of griffins guarding gold in the far north, against the one-eyed Arimaspians who tried to steal it. Dungeons & Dragons spells it "griffon" and makes it a horse-hunter whose young are worth a fortune:

    They are aggressive carnivores, and their favorite prey are horses.
    - Monster Manual (1977), p. 50

    Fledglings sell for 5,000 gold pieces, eggs for 2,000, on the open market.
    - Monster Manual (1977), p. 50

[#f5e342]Rogue Trivia[-]

The griffin is new in Rogue 5.x, where it is one of the few monsters with three special powers at once: it is mean, it flies, and it regenerates. Its 2000 experience is the third highest in the game, behind only the jabberwock (3000) and the dragon (5000); the next monster down, the vampire, is worth 350. In rx1 its wingbeats can also hurl the hero back, and it calls down lightning.

The description at the top comes from the Macintosh version of Rogue.
